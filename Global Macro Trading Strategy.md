Here is a comprehensive, module-by-module breakdown to construct a macro-driven, top-down strategy modeled after the PFTM (Professional Forex Trading Masterclass) framework.

Since executing complex trading frameworks requires flexibility, this guide is optimized for cloud-based tracking and mobile-friendly charting apps (like Google Sheets and the TradingView app), allowing you to maintain and execute the strategy entirely on portable devices.

Skill File 1: The Macroeconomic Scorecard (The Foundation)
The core of this strategy is not guessing price direction, but measuring the fundamental strength or weakness of an economy. You will build a scorecard assigning a +1 (Bullish), 0 (Neutral), or -1 (Bearish) to major economies (US, Eurozone, UK, Japan, Australia).

Step-by-Step Setup:

Create a Cloud Spreadsheet: Open a new workbook in a cloud-based app like Google Sheets. This ensures your dashboard syncs automatically and is fully operable on the go without requiring desktop-bound add-ins.

Track the Big Four Indicators: Set up rows for the four main drivers of currency valuation:

GDP Growth (QoQ): Is the economy expanding? (+1 for beating expectations/trend, -1 for missing).

Inflation (CPI YoY): High inflation usually forces central banks to raise rates.

Interest Rates: Central bank base rates. Higher yields attract foreign capital (+1).

Employment: Unemployment rate trends.

Establish the Net Score: Sum the values for each country. A country with rising GDP, sticky inflation, and rising rates might score a +3. A country with contracting GDP and falling rates scores a -3.

Skill File 2: Asset Class Application (Cross-Asset Matrix)
Once your economies are scored, you apply these fundamental biases to different asset classes.

Step-by-Step Setup:

Major FX Pairs (The Divergence Trade): Buy the currency of the strongest economy against the currency of the weakest. If the US scores +3 and Japan scores -3, your fundamental bias is heavily LONG USD/JPY. Never pair a +1 with a +1; you need fundamental divergence.

Equity Indexes (Risk-On vs. Risk-Off):

If global GDP is rising and unemployment is low (Risk-On), you look for long positions in major indexes like the S&P 500 or NASDAQ.

If central banks are aggressively hiking rates to fight inflation while GDP slows (Stagflation/Risk-Off), the bias on indexes shifts to short.

Commodities (Supply/Demand & USD Inverse):

Gold: Fundamentally driven by real interest rates (Interest rate minus inflation). If real yields are negative, Gold gets a +1.

Oil/Copper: Driven by global PMI (manufacturing) data. If global PMIs are expanding (>50), demand for industrial commodities gets a +1. Remember to price in the strength of the US Dollar, as commodities are priced in USD.

Skill File 3: Technical Execution & Timing
Fundamentals tell you what to trade and which direction. Technicals tell you when to enter. Do not take a trade unless the technical trend aligns with your macro scorecard.

Step-by-Step Setup:

Set Up Your Charting Environment: Use a robust mobile charting application. Set your primary timeframe to the Daily (1D) chart for trend identification, dropping to the 4-Hour (4H) for precise entries.

Trend Confirmation: Ensure price is making Higher Highs and Higher Lows (for a macro long) or Lower Highs and Lower Lows (for a macro short).

Triggering the Entry: Wait for price action to offer a favorable risk-to-reward ratio. Applying a volatility-based indicator like the Keltner Channel is highly effective here.

Breakout Strategy: If a currency pair has been consolidating but the fundamental scorecard just shifted to a strong divergence (e.g., +3 vs -2), look for price to break and close outside the upper Keltner Channel to signal the start of the macro-driven momentum.

Pullback Strategy: If the macro trend is already established, wait for price to pull back toward the moving average (the middle line of the Keltner Channel) to enter at a discount in the direction of your fundamental bias.

Skill File 4: Risk & Portfolio Management
Institutional traders do not blow up their accounts on a single trade. Risk management is non-negotiable.

Step-by-Step Setup:

Calculate Position Size: Never risk more than 1% to 2% of your total account capital on a single trade idea.

Determine the Stop Loss: Place your stop loss based on market volatility, not arbitrary dollar amounts. Use the Average True Range (ATR) indicator. A common institutional standard is placing the stop loss 1.5 to 2 ATRs away from the entry price to avoid being stopped out by normal daily noise.

Manage Correlation: If your macro scorecard tells you the USD is fundamentally strong, you might want to short EUR/USD, short GBP/USD, and long USD/JPY. Warning: These are all essentially the exact same trade (Long USD). If you take all three, you are tripling your risk. Choose the single pair with the largest fundamental divergence and only execute that one.
