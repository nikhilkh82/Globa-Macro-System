# Technical Analysis & Price Action

**What it is & why it matters** — Technical analysis is the Global Macros strategy's *timing and execution* layer. Once macro and statistical work has selected an asset and a direction, price action across multiple timeframes — trend, support/resistance, and moving-average context — is used to refine entries and exits and to confirm or veto a setup. The strategy treats TA as secondary to fundamentals but indispensable for execution: it pairs directly with [[Average True Range (ATR)]] (how far price moves) and [[Distribution of Returns]] (how returns are shaped) to place volatility-aware [[Risk Management]] stops and targets at structurally sensible price levels.

## Charts & key trends
All figures are historical, as captured in the datasets.

- **AUD/USD multi-timeframe study (`AUD-USD-Technical_Analysis`, Sep 2008 → May 2015):** the workbook is built for **top-down price-action analysis** — the same instrument charted on three progressively shorter windows (whole sample → 3-year → 1-year) so the trader reads the major trend, then the intermediate trend, then the entry timeframe.
  - Over the full sample AUD/USD ranged from a **0.6052 low** (the 2008 crisis trough) to a **1.1032 high** (the post-QE commodity-currency peak above parity), mean ≈ **0.9232**.
  - The series shows the classic AUD arc: crash to ~0.60 in late 2008, multi-year uptrend to >1.10 by 2011–2013, then a sustained downtrend back to **~0.79 by May 2015** — a textbook macro trend visible across all three timeframe panes.
  - Daily % change ranged **−7.06% to +7.40%** (mean ≈ 0%), consistent with the AUDUSD volatility documented in [[Average True Range (ATR)]] and [[Distribution of Returns]].
- **SMA vs EMA (`SMA vs EMA.xls`, Apr–May 2021, 30 daily prices):** a compact teaching example contrasting the two most-used moving averages on a series running **46.02 → 63.70** (mean 54.57).
  - **10-day SMA** (simple average of the last 10 closes) finishes at **59.20**; the **10-day EMA** finishes slightly higher at **59.34**, having tracked the recent rally faster.
  - The EMA's **smoothing constant = 2/(10+1) = 0.1818** is shown explicitly — each new EMA = price·0.1818 + prior EMA·0.8182, so the EMA weights recent prices more heavily and turns sooner than the lagging SMA. This is the practical reason a Global Macros trader uses an EMA for responsive trend/entry signals and an SMA for a smoother trend backbone.

## How it's used in the strategy
- **Top-down / multi-timeframe confirmation.** The AUD/USD workbook embodies the method: establish the dominant trend on the long chart, locate the swing on the medium chart, time the entry on the short chart. A trade is only taken when the timeframes agree with the macro thesis.
- **Trend filters via moving averages.** SMA/EMA define trend direction and dynamic support/resistance; the faster EMA flags trend changes earlier (the smoothing-constant lesson), while the SMA filters noise. Crossovers and price-vs-MA position act as entry/exit and bias filters.
- **Structural stop/target placement.** Price-action levels (prior swing highs/lows, support/resistance) anchor *where* stops and targets sit, while [[Average True Range (ATR)]] sets *how far* they sit from entry — the two combine in [[Risk Management]] so stops clear noise yet rest beyond meaningful structure.
- **Execution, not selection.** TA sharpens timing on ideas already generated by macro/statistical work — it confirms or vetoes, it does not originate the directional view ([[Trade Idea Generation Process]], [[The Global Macros Framework]]).
