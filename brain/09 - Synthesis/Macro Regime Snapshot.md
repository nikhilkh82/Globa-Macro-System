# Macro Regime Snapshot

**What it is & why it matters** — This is the cross-asset assembly point of the Global Macro Brain: it takes the scored reads from growth, inflation, rates, FX, positioning and volatility and resolves them into a single regime call and the long/short tilt the Global Macros framework implies. It is the practical destination of the top-down process described in [[The Global Macros Framework]] and the launchpad for the [[Trade Idea Generation Process]].

> **CRITICAL VINTAGE WARNING.** This snapshot is stitched together from a *historical teaching corpus* whose series carry **mixed vintages between roughly 2013 and 2022**. It is **NOT** a description of live markets as of today (2026-06-18). Different signals below were captured at different times — the COT/positioning reads are late-2017, the FX scorecards are 2015/2017, the yield-curve slope history ends 2014, the inflation prints run to Oct-2022. Read every figure as "what the dataset shows, data through <date>," and read the regime call as *an illustration of how the Global Macros framework synthesises a cross-asset picture*, not as actionable current positioning. See **Data vintage & caveats** at the bottom before drawing any conclusion.

## The regime in one line
Across the corpus the framework's signals describe a **late-cycle, liquidity-driven expansion with rebuilding inflation pressure**: leading indicators V-shaped back above their expansion lines, coincident inflation surging then just beginning to roll over, rates pinned near the zero bound under a steep (non-recessionary) curve, an absolute-inflationary USD that is nonetheless relatively strong against weaker currencies, and crowded "short-vol / short-JPY / long-AUD" speculative positioning sitting on a compressed VIX — i.e. risk-on with embedded fragility.

## 1. Growth signal — PMIs vs GDP
The leading layer ([[Leading Indicators]]) shows a sharp **V-shaped recovery back into expansion**: the ISM Manufacturing PMI's last six readings run **53.7 → 55.6 → 55.7 → 58.8 → 57.7 → 60.5 (Dec-2020)**, well above the 50 expansion line (long-run avg ~52.9, range 29.4–77.5). Services confirm — the NMI averages ~54.3 and ends at 57.7. Because the S&P 500 *leads* US real GDP by ~2–3 quarters (rolling correlation ~0.31 contemporaneous → ~0.57 at a 6-month lag, per [[GDP & Growth]]), a PMI rising off a sub-50 trough is historically the most bullish equity backdrop. The GDP series itself shows the whipsaw the PMI front-ran: real GDP QoQ-annualised swung from **−31.4% to +33.4%** through 2020. Coincident hard data ([[Coincident Indicators]]) confirms the rebound — non-farm payrolls swung from a record **−20,679k (Apr-2020)** collapse to **+4,846k**, unemployment retracing from its **14.8% (Apr-2020)** all-time high to **5.8% (May-2021)**.

**Read:** growth signal = **expansion / recovery**, broad enough to support a pro-cyclical tilt, but late-stage (UoM consumer sentiment and NFIB optimism had already peaked and were rolling over in places).

## 2. Inflation
The coincident inflation set ([[Coincident Indicators]]) is the dataset's most dramatic story: headline CPI y/y **peaked at 9.0% (June 2022)** — the highest in decades — and was just beginning to roll over to **7.76% by Oct 2022** (the first disinflation in the corpus). Core CPI peaked **6.66%**, Energy y/y **+41.5%**, Food y/y **~10.9%**. Upstream, **PPI All Commodities** (the pipeline tell, history to 1913) is far wilder, y/y range **−43.9% to +47.2%**. Note the vintage split: the **Core PCE** read in the older workbook is a subdued **1.53% y/y (Jan-2021)** versus the 2022 CPI surge — the two capture *different moments* of the same inflation wave. The liquidity precursor is unambiguous ([[M2 Money Supply & Liquidity]]): US M2 YoY hit an **all-time-sample high of +25.8% into Jan-2021** (long-run mean ~7.0%) — the money surge that preceded the 2022 inflation print.

**Read:** inflation signal = **high and (at the latest vintage) just past peak**, with the M2 surge confirming a liquidity-driven impulse. This is consistent with the FX endo scorecard's "inflationary overall" tag.

## 3. Rates & the curve
The rate regime ([[Government Bond Yields]]) is **ultra-low / zero-bound**: as captured (data thru 2021-01-03) Fed Funds sat at **0.09%**, the 10yr at **0.93%**, the 30yr at **1.66%**, and US real (TIPS) yields were deeply negative (10yr −1.03%, 5yr −1.55%). The curve was **steep and non-recessionary** — the 10yr-2yr spread ~**+0.81pp** on the 2021 snapshot. The longer slope history ([[Yield Curve & Recession Signals]], 10yr-3mo, 2000→2014) ranged **−0.95pp to +3.85pp** (mean +1.95pp); its only inversion (the **−0.95pp** trough) was the **2006–07 pre-GFC** signal — *not* present in the latest-vintage data. A positive/steepening curve is the framework's "risk-on / expansion" lean.

**Read:** rates signal = **easy policy, steep curve, deeply negative real rates** — supportive of risk assets and consistent with the M2/inflation picture (the Fed sponsoring growth via low rates and QE). No curve-inversion recession flag in the latest data.

## 4. FX bias — endogenous / exogenous
The FX engine ([[FX Endogenous-Exogenous Framework]]) reads the **US endogenous score at +36** on a −150…+160 scale = **"inflationary overall"** (leading sub-total +11, inflation +10, sovereign/CA +25; Govt Debt/GDP 102.5% scored +10, US 10y 2.14% scored +7). On an *absolute* basis that means the USD is losing purchasing power. But the *relative* (exogenous) layer flips it for the cross: the **AUD/USD exo score is −14** (relative GDP −2, relative BOP −6, rate-diff/carry −4, relative wealth −2). The combiner resolves to **Total USD/AUD +22 (long-USD bias) / Total AUD/USD −14 (short-AUD bias)** — the AUD is losing purchasing power *faster* than the USD. The dominant AUD driver is Chinese commodity demand (iron ore = 22% of Australian exports, China = 29% of export market); iron ore had collapsed from **$197.12 (Apr-2008)** toward **$68 (Feb-2015)** and AUD/USD tracked it down.

**Read:** FX bias = **absolutely inflationary USD but relatively strong vs weaker commodity currencies** → the worked pair resolves **short AUD/USD**. The general lesson: in this regime, score each currency's debasement, then go long the one debasing slower.

## 5. Positioning (COT) & the volatility regime
Positioning ([[Commitment of Traders (COT)]], data to late-2017) shows the **crowded configurations the framework flags as fragility**: speculators are **chronically net-short volatility** (VIX Flip mean −18.4; latest −13.6 with VIX at **10.17**, near the 9.36–28.03 lows) — the structural short-vol carry trade that later detonated in the Feb-2018 "Volmageddon." They are **near the most extreme net-short-JPY reading** (USD/JPY Flip −37.0, a crowded-carry warning) and **heavily net-long AUD** (Flip +40.0, upper third of the historic range — note this *contradicts* the framework's short-AUD fundamental bias, exactly the kind of crowded-against-thesis setup Global Macros sizes down). The S&P Flip is structurally negative (range −13.6 to −1.4, latest −3.0 with the index at 2500 after a near-monotonic bull run).

The volatility regime ([[VIX & Implied Volatility]]) is **compressed / complacent**: the VIX spends the corpus low (teens-to-low-20s, ~22.96 at the chart's end) and is strongly inversely correlated with the S&P (5yr rolling correlation ~**−0.7 to −0.85**). A low VIX with deeply net-short vol positioning is the textbook complacency-plus-fragility combination — calm on the surface, gap-risk underneath.

**Read:** positioning/vol signal = **risk-on consensus with embedded tail risk** — crowded short-vol, short-JPY and long-AUD, on a pinned VIX. The framework treats this as a reason to size carefully, not to chase.

## What long/short tilt the framework implies
Assembling the layers, the Global Macros framework's implied stance in this (historical) regime is a **pro-cyclical, net-long-but-hedged** book:
- **Net exposure:** modestly net long — expansionary PMIs, easy policy, steep curve and a rising equity trend support risk, but late-cycle peaking sentiment and crowded positioning argue against full aggression. (Consistent with the modestly net-long, beta-hedged model book in [[Portfolio Management]].)
- **Sectors:** tilt toward **cyclicals over defensives** while PMIs rise off the trough; the M2/inflation surge favours real-asset and inflation-beneficiary tilts — routed through [[Sector Analysis & Rotation]].
- **FX:** **long USD vs the weaker commodity/funding currencies** (worked example short AUD/USD), respecting that long-AUD positioning is already crowded against the fundamental bias.
- **Rates:** a steep, easy curve argues against a recession-hedge stance; the disinflation just starting at the latest vintage (CPI 9.0%→7.76%) is the pivot to watch.
- **Risk overlay:** the compressed VIX + net-short-vol positioning is the dominant fragility flag — keep beta-hedges on and stops at low-probability return levels per [[Risk Management]] and [[Distribution of Returns]]; do not be short volatility into complacency.

## Data vintage & caveats
- **This is historical teaching data, not live markets.** Today is 2026-06-18; the corpus vintages span ~2013–2022. Nothing here describes current conditions.
- **The signals are not contemporaneous with each other.** COT/positioning ≈ late-2017; VIX context ≈ 2017 with charts to 2021–2022; FX endo scorecard ≈ 2017, AUD/USD exo scorecard ≈ Q1-2015; yield-curve slope history ends 2014 while the curve *level* snapshot is Jan–Feb-2021; CPI/PPI run to Oct-2022; M2 to Jan-2021. The "snapshot" is therefore a **composite illustration of method**, not a single dated market state.
- **Reverse-chronological sources.** Several underlying workbooks store the newest row on top; "latest" readings above were taken accordingly (flagged on the source pages).
- **The regime call is illustrative.** It shows *how* the framework fuses cross-asset signals into a tilt — not a recommendation. Re-run the process on live data before acting.
- **Known internal tension (a feature, not a bug):** the fundamental short-AUD/USD bias coexists with crowded net-long-AUD COT positioning — exactly the conflict the Global Macros process is designed to surface and size around.
