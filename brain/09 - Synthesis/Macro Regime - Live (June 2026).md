## Regime in one line
A **late-cycle expansion in which the energy spike that both central banks tightened into has, a week later, unwound.** The Fed's **first hike of the cycle** — 25bp to a **3.75–4.00%** target range, effective **17 Sep** — is holding: EFFR **3.88%** every day from 18 to **22 Sep** (`DFF`), against an August monthly average of 3.63% (`FEDFUNDS`). The ECB's deposit rate is **2.50%** (raised from 2.25%, effective 16 Sep). **But the shock they leaned against is deflating**: WTI **$96.41** on 22 Sep (`DCOILWTICO`), **$10.61 below** the $107.02 spike high of 15 Sep, and Brent **$114.89** (`DCOILBRENTEU`) from **$130.80** — the year-over-year impulse has cooled from +68.1% / +92.7% to **+53.1% / +71.8%**, still enormous, but no longer re-intensifying. August PPI all-commodities is **+9.85%** y/y (+0.96% m/m, `PPIACO`) — an August observation that **predates the unwind** and therefore now overstates the live impulse. **Demand is firm, not fading**: August retail sales +1.24% m/m (**+6.01%** y/y), payrolls **+162k** (3-month average **+71k**, U-3 **4.1%**, participation **61.6%**), initial claims **196k** (12 Sep), industrial production **+1.42%** y/y. The inflation read is unchanged — no new CPI or PCE print has landed since the 16-Sep refresh: core CPI **+2.45%** y/y (Jul +2.47%) with a 3-month annualised pace of **1.97%** (from 1.64%) on a +0.29% m/m August, **core PCE still +3.34%** (Jul; 3m annualised 3.05%), headline CPI **+3.35%** y/y (+0.40% m/m) on a 3m annualised pace of only **+0.18%**. The curve is **little changed and still positively sloped**: 10Y-2Y **+0.26** (23 Sep) after touching **+0.20** on 21 Sep, 10Y-3M **+0.92** (23 Sep, from +0.87), 2Y **4.71%** / 10Y **4.96%** (22 Sep), 10Y real **2.63%**. And the tape did more than absorb the hike — it rallied through it: VIX **14.21** (22 Sep), *below* the 15.44 of hike day; IG / HY / CCC OAS all tighter at **0.77 / 2.68 / 10.75**; S&P 500 **7,706.03** (23 Sep, +15.76% y/y) after a 7,764.70 close on 21 Sep. The FX cells now clear both hikes for the first time, and the dollar firmed into them: broad USD **119.51** (18 Sep), y/y back to **−0.12%** from −1.45%. Net: growth intact, policy leaning against an energy impulse that is now deflating on its own, and a risk tape that has priced the tightening as benign.

## Implied tilt (illustrative)
- **Net exposure:** still pro-cyclical, and the case got *stronger* this week — firm demand (retail sales +6.01% y/y, payrolls +162k, claims 196k), a positively-sloped curve, credit tighter at every tier (IG 0.77 / HY 2.68 / CCC 10.75 on 22 Sep) and a bull tape. The tape did not merely absorb the Fed's first hike, it **rallied through it**: VIX 17.71 (16 Sep) → 15.44 (17 Sep) → **14.21** (22 Sep), the S&P from 7,637.76 to **7,706.03** (23 Sep). The risk is intact; it is now carried against a Fed that is tightening, not one that is waiting — and against an energy shock that is deflating without further help.
- **Inflation-aware — this is the leg that changed.** The pass-through question is still answered only at the margin (no new CPI or PCE since the 16-Sep read: core CPI 3m annualised 1.97% against a flat +2.45% YoY, core PCE +3.34% still a July figure), **but the commodity impulse driving the reflation tilt has reversed**: WTI $107.02 → **$96.41**, Brent $130.80 → **$114.89**. August PPI's +9.85% is now a lagging witness to a spike that has passed, its own 3m pace already −3.1%, and the 5y breakeven sits at **2.34%**. Real-asset and inflation-beneficiary tilts ([[Cyclical Commodities]]) are still the expression of the *level* impulse — copper +38.6% y/y is the cleanest survivor — but the momentum case for adding to them has gone, and the next genuine test remains the **August core PCE**, not another CPI.
- **Rates:** the market had been tightening on the Fed's behalf (real 10Y **2.63%**, +63bp since end-March) and the Fed has now joined it, but **the flattening stalled this week**: 2s10s **+0.26** (23 Sep) against +0.25 on 18 Sep, after dipping to +0.20 on 21 Sep, while 10Y-3M widened to **+0.92** — all of it on 23 Sep, and on spread evidence only (see the Rates row). A recession hedge is still not the trade. The flattener has not resumed paying either — at +0.26 with the front end pinned by a 3.88% EFFR, the box is close enough to flat that the asymmetry is in the 10Y-3M leg, not in 2s10s.
- **FX — the first post-hike evidence is in, and it favours the dollar.** The framework stays split on AUD/USD (card −4, template +2) while specs lean only mildly short (AUD Flip −7.9%). But the H.10 cells now reach **18 Sep**: broad USD **119.51**, y/y from −1.45% to **−0.12%**; EUR/USD 1.1464; AUD/USD 0.7111. The crowded dollar long was trimmed again (+30.4% → +24.2%) *into* a dollar that then firmed — the divergence is closing from the price side, not the positioning side. And **USD/JPY 156.87** is the sharper warning: the yen is weaker on the week specs held their largest net long of the cycle (+22.2%, 84th percentile). Size against the crowd, not with it ([[Risk Management]]).
- **Fragility:** VIX **14.21** with a still-deep short-vol book (−19.4%), HY OAS 2.68 but CCC-and-lower at **10.75** — 129bp wider than 1 Jun. The configuration is unchanged in kind but **more stretched in degree**: the compensation for holding it has fallen (vol 1.2 points lower, spreads tighter) while the tail that would break it — the CCC tier and the short-vol book — is the same size. The 18-Sep CCC wobble to 10.83 reversing within two sessions is the tell that this is latent, not gone. Watch CCC and the short-vol book together; they are the two places this configuration usually breaks first.
