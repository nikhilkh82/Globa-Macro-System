# Current Cross-Country Snapshot (Fragility & Twin Deficits)

This is the **2024 cross-section** of the [[00 - GMD Overview & Structural Edge|Global Macro Database]]: a four-pillar fragility scorecard across **190 countries** plus the **twin-deficit map** (97 of 192 countries), built to flag the sovereign-stress / EM-underweight watchlist for the book.

## What this page is

The historical pages in this folder ([[01 - Where Today Sits vs History (US & G7 Percentiles)]], [[02 - Crisis Chronology (Banking, Currency, Sovereign)]]) tell you *what has happened*. This page is the **live photograph of where the world's balance sheets and external accounts stand right now**, ranked by vulnerability. It is the screening layer that sits in front of country-level due diligence in `12 - Major Economies Analysis`.

---

## Pillar 1 — The 2024 fragility scorecard (P5)

The scorecard scores every country with a complete **quad** of indicators for 2024 and collapses them into a single cross-sectional z-score. Coverage: **190 countries** with the full set:

| Pillar | Variable | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General-government **debt/GDP** | Stock vulnerability — how much is owed |
| 2 | **Fiscal balance** | Flow vulnerability — is the hole getting deeper |
| 3 | **Current account** | External vulnerability — reliance on foreign financing |
| 4 | **Inflation** | Monetary / nominal-anchor stress |

The composite z is **signed so that higher = more fragile**. The most-vulnerable cluster in 2024 is led by:

> **Sudan · Timor-Leste · Lebanon · Venezuela · Argentina**

These are the names where multiple pillars light up at once — the cross-section's tail, not a single bad print.

### The inflation pillar, in context

The 2024 cross-country inflation distribution shows just how skewed the tail is:

| Statistic | 2024 inflation |
|---|---|
| Median | **3.2%** |
| 90th percentile | **21.2%** |
| Maximum | **736%** (Zimbabwe) |

The median country is near a normal ~3% print, but the p90-to-max gap is enormous: a handful of monetary blow-ups (Zimbabwe at the extreme) drag the tail out by two orders of magnitude. This is why inflation enters the scorecard as a *fragility* signal, not just a cyclical one — see the currency-collapse history in [[02 - Crisis Chronology (Banking, Currency, Sovereign)]].

## Pillar 2 — The twin-deficit map (P10)

A separate, cleaner cut overlays **fiscal balance against current-account balance** for **192 countries** in 2024. The four quadrants are the map:

| Quadrant | Fiscal | Current account | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top-right | Surplus | Surplus | **Creditor / safe haven** |
| Bottom-left | Deficit | **Deficit** | **Twin-deficit — external-financing vulnerable** |
| Off-diagonal | Mixed | Mixed | One leg of support |

The headline number: **97 of the 192 countries run BOTH a fiscal deficit and a current-account deficit.** That is the external-financing vulnerability set — economies that must simultaneously fund the government *and* the country from outside, leaving them most exposed to a rollover squeeze, a funding-cost shock, or a sudden stop.

The mirror image is the **top-right creditor-surplus cluster** — the safe havens that export capital rather than import it, and that historically absorb flight-to-quality flows when the twin-deficit set comes under stress.

## How the two pillars combine

The fragility scorecard (P5) and the twin-deficit map (P10) are complementary, not redundant:

- The **twin-deficit map** is a binary, transparent first screen — *is this country structurally dependent on foreign financing?* (Yes for 97 of 192.)
- The **scorecard** then adds the **debt stock** and **inflation** pillars, so a country can be flagged even if its external account is balanced (e.g., a high-debt, high-inflation closed economy) or can be partially excused if its creditor surplus offsets a fiscal hole.

The watchlist names worth the most scrutiny are those that appear in **both**: a bottom-left twin-deficit position *and* a top-cluster scorecard z. Argentina is the archetype in the 2024 cross-section.

## Cross-reference: the historical anchors

This snapshot inherits the base rates from the rest of the folder. Three are load-bearing for interpreting any flag:

- **There is no universal "safe" debt level.** Across 181 sovereign defaults, the median debt/GDP *at default* was just **61%** (IQR **32–94%**), and many defaults occurred below 60% while many high-debt sovereigns never defaulted. Default is about willingness, rollover, and FX-denomination — not a threshold. See [[03 - Crisis Aftermath & Recovery Base Rates]].
- **Debt drags forward growth.** Pooled across 197 countries (n=13,374), forward-5y real growth steps down by starting-debt bucket — **<30%: 3.57%**, **60–90%: 2.91%**, **>120%: 2.77%** — with a clear break above 60%. A high-debt flag on the scorecard implies a slower growth path, not just a higher default odds.
- **The DM/EM line is blurring.** DM public debt/GDP went **71.4%→102.1%** (1995→2024) while EM went **45.2%→74.6%** — advanced economies are converging *up* toward historically-EM levels, so "developed" is no longer a clean exemption from the scorecard.

## What it means for positioning

- **Underweight the bottom-left twin-deficit set.** The 97 dual-deficit economies are the natural EM-underweight / sovereign-stress watchlist — funding-cost shocks and dollar strength hit them first and hardest. Cross-check against [[USD & G10 FX]] and [[Government Bond Yields]] for the funding-cost side.
- **Treat the scorecard tail (Sudan, Timor-Leste, Lebanon, Venezuela, Argentina) as no-touch / tactical-only.** Multiple pillars firing at once is the signature that precedes the crises catalogued in [[02 - Crisis Chronology (Banking, Currency, Sovereign)]].
- **Use the inflation tail as a hard filter.** Median 3.2% is benign; the p90 of 21.2% and the 736% max mark where nominal anchors have failed. Anything materially above p90 is a currency/monetary-regime story, not a rates trade.
- **Lean to top-right creditor-surplus economies as the safe-haven leg** of a relative-value sovereign book, especially into risk-off. Position sizing should respect [[Risk Management]].
- **Pull individual names into deep-dive.** This page is the screen; country-level fundamentals, FRED-based time series, and policy detail live in `12 - Major Economies Analysis`. The US-specific cut is in `11 - USA Country Analysis`; cycle/asset-return context is in `13 - Macro Cycles & Asset Returns`.

For the current top-down stance into which these flags feed, see [[Macro Regime - Live (June 2026)]] and [[Macro Regime Snapshot]]; the dial-and-screen view is in the [Global Macro Database Dashboard](Global%20Macro%20Database%20Dashboard.html).
